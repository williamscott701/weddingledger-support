1. The short version

Wedding Ledger doesn't collect your data, because Wedding Ledger doesn't collect data. Everything you enter — your budget, your categories, every vendor, every payment, every note and phone number — is written into Wedding Ledger's own private storage area on your iPhone or iPad: the same sandboxed, on-device storage most apps use, protected by your device's passcode, Face ID or Touch ID like everything else on your phone. There is no account for it to be attached to, and no server for it to travel to. If you delete Wedding Ledger, that data is deleted with it. Nobody at William Scott can see what you enter, because it never reaches us in the first place.

The one thing on your device that ever talks to a server is the App Store's own billing system, when you buy the Full Suite — that is covered in "Purchases and payment" below.

2. Financial information, and what Wedding Ledger is not

A wedding budget is, by its nature, a detailed record of what you can afford and what you are spending it on. Wedding Ledger treats it accordingly: it stays on your device, it is never transmitted, and every total in the app is calculated on your own phone from your own entries.

Wedding Ledger does not connect to your bank. There is no Plaid, no Open Banking, no card linking and no transaction import. Every figure in the app is one you typed in yourself. The app has no ability to move money, initiate a payment, or see any account you hold.

Wedding Ledger is a record-keeping tool, not financial advice. It adds up what you tell it and shows you where you stand against the budget you set. It does not recommend what to spend, does not advise on borrowing, and is not a substitute for advice from someone qualified who knows your circumstances.

3. No accounts, no sign-in

Wedding Ledger doesn't ask you to sign up, sign in, or create a profile. There is no username, no password, and no email address collected by the app, and no server-side account of any kind for your information to attach to. Nothing you do in Wedding Ledger is tied to your identity anywhere but on your own device.

The app does ask, optionally, for the couple's names during setup. They are used for two things: a greeting on the countdown widget, and a heading on the PDF report you might share with whoever is contributing. You can leave the field blank or change it at any time in Settings.

4. What Wedding Ledger stores, and where

Everything below is stored locally, using Apple's on-device SwiftData framework, inside Wedding Ledger's own private storage area on your device. None of it is transmitted to us, to Apple, or to anyone else — Wedding Ledger has no networking code that could send it anywhere, aside from the App Store billing system described later in this policy.

4.1 Your wedding

The couple's names if you enter them, the wedding date if you set one, your total budget, your guest count, and your chosen currency.

4.2 Budget categories

The name of each category, the icon you picked for it, and the amount you planned for it.

4.3 Vendors

For each supplier: the name, the category, where they are in your pipeline, the amount quoted, the amount contracted, the date you booked them, and any contact details or free-text notes you choose to enter.

4.4 Payments

For each payment: what it is for, the amount, the date it is due, the date you marked it paid if you have, the payment method if you record one, and an optional note.

4.5 Your app settings

A handful of small preferences — whether you have finished setup, your reminder lead time and hour, and whether reminders are on — stored using iOS's standard on-device settings storage, the same mechanism nearly every app uses for simple preferences. These stay on your device too.

Free-text notes are exactly that: genuinely blank until you type into them, with nothing read, scanned, or analysed by anything, and stored as you wrote them.

5. Vendor contact details

You can type a phone number, an email address or a website against a vendor. Those are stored with the vendor, on your device, like everything else.

Wedding Ledger never reads your Contacts. The app does not request access to your address book, has no permission to do so, and cannot import from it. Anything in a vendor's contact fields is there because you typed or pasted it.

Tapping Call, Email or Website on a vendor hands that number, address or link to iOS, which opens it in your phone app, mail app or browser. That is a normal hand-off between apps on your device; nothing is transmitted to us, and the app has no visibility into what happens after the hand-off.

6. Widgets

If you add a Wedding Ledger widget to your Home Screen or Lock Screen, the app writes a small summary — your countdown, your budget totals and your next payment due — into a storage area shared between the Wedding Ledger app and its widget. Both run on your own device. This exists purely so the widget, which runs as its own lightweight process without access to the app's full database, can show something useful without opening the app. That shared summary never leaves your device: it is not a sync feature, and there is no version of it that reaches a server, iCloud, or another device you own.

Bear in mind that a Lock Screen widget is visible to anyone who picks up your phone. If you would rather your next payment amount were not on the lock screen, use the Home Screen widgets or none at all.

7. Notifications

Wedding Ledger's payment reminders are local notifications, scheduled by the app on your device and delivered by iOS. They are not push notifications: nothing is sent from a server, and no device token, identifier or notification content ever leaves your phone. If you decline the notification permission, or turn reminders off later, the app simply schedules nothing.

A reminder does name the vendor and the amount due, because a reminder that said only "a payment is due" would be useless. If you would rather that did not appear on your lock screen, iOS lets you hide notification previews for individual apps in its own Settings.

8. Purchases and payment

The Full Suite is sold through Apple's App Store, using Apple's own in-app purchase system, either as an auto-renewing subscription or as a one-time lifetime unlock. That transaction happens entirely between you and Apple.

We never see, receive, or store your name, your payment card, your billing address, or your Apple Account details. What the app receives from Apple's StoreKit framework is a signed receipt saying whether this device's Apple Account currently has access, and when a subscription renews or expires. Wedding Ledger reads that to decide whether to unlock the Full Suite features, and it is not linked to anything you have entered.

The lifetime unlock is Family Shareable, so whoever you are marrying can use the same purchase. Family Sharing is handled entirely by Apple; the app is told only that access applies, never who the other person is.

Apple's handling of the purchase is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy, not by this one. Apple provides the developer with anonymised, aggregated sales reports — how many purchases were made in a country, for instance — which contain no information about individual customers and cannot be connected to any person or to any budget.

9. Exporting and sharing your data

The Full Suite unlocks a PDF report and CSV exports of your budget, vendors and payment schedule. All of them are created on your device. Where they go afterwards is entirely your decision: the iOS share sheet hands the file to whatever you pick — Mail, Messages, Files, AirDrop, a printer, another app. Once you send it somewhere, that destination's own privacy terms apply to the copy you sent, and it is outside our reach. Nothing is exported automatically, and nothing is ever sent to us.

A wedding budget report names your suppliers and what you are paying them. Think about that before sending one to a group chat.

10. Device backups

If you back up your device to iCloud or to a computer, your Wedding Ledger data is included in that backup like any other app's. Those backups are Apple's, governed by Apple's terms and encryption, and they exist so that your budget survives a lost phone. We have no access to them, and no ability to read, restore, or delete one. If you would rather your budget were not in a backup, iOS lets you exclude individual apps from iCloud Backup in its own Settings.

11. No tracking, analytics, or advertising

Wedding Ledger contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting service, no advertising, no advertising identifier, and no third-party code that reports on how you use the app. Nothing tracks you across apps or websites, so the App Tracking Transparency prompt never appears — there is nothing for it to ask about. The app's Privacy Manifest, which Apple requires and publishes on the App Store product page as "Data Not Collected", says the same thing in Apple's own format.

The wedding industry is unusually aggressive about selling couples' details to vendors. Wedding Ledger has no way to participate in that, and never will: there is no list to sell, because there is no list.

12. Deleting your data

Two ways, both immediate and both entirely in your hands. Settings → Erase all data inside the app erases every category, vendor and payment while keeping the app installed. Deleting the app from your device removes the app and its storage together.

Because nothing is stored off-device, there is no copy anywhere for us to delete on your behalf, and no way for us or anyone else to recover it once it is gone. Export what you want to keep before you delete.

13. Your rights, wherever you are

Wedding Ledger is available worldwide, and privacy laws differ from country to country. Rights such as access, correction, deletion, portability and objection — under the EU and UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act, Brazil's LGPD, India's DPDP Act, and comparable laws elsewhere — all concern personal data held by a company about you.

In Wedding Ledger's case there is nothing for those requests to reach: we hold no personal data about you at all. In practice you already exercise every one of those rights directly, without asking anyone. Access and portability are the CSV and PDF exports; correction is editing an entry; deletion is the button in Settings or deleting the app. There is no profiling, no automated decision-making with legal effects, and no sale or sharing of personal information — we have none to sell. If you would like this confirmed in writing for your own records, email us and we will confirm it.

14. Children's privacy

Wedding Ledger is intended for adults planning a wedding, and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age — there is no mechanism in the app by which we could. A parent or guardian who wants a child's data removed can delete it from the device directly, using either method in section 12.

15. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new "last updated" date. Because the app collects nothing, any change is likely to be a clarification rather than a new use of your data. If that ever stopped being true — if a future version of Wedding Ledger needed to send something off your device, for shared planning between two phones, say — it would be described here plainly, before it shipped, and it would be something you opt into.
